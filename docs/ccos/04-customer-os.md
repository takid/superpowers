# 04 — Customer OS

## What it owns

Everything after the first clean. Onboarding, the conversion from one-off to recurring,
retention, reviews, upsell, referral, win-back, and the decision about which customers are
worth keeping.

Sales owns getting them. This module owns keeping them, and keeping them is where the
money actually is.

## The rule that makes this work

The recurring conversion happens in the 48 hours after the first clean, or it does not
happen.

This is the single highest-leverage window in the business. The customer has just walked
into a clean house. The difference between what it was and what it is now is sitting right
in front of them, and it will never be that vivid again. Two weeks later the feeling has
faded and the decision becomes abstract.

Most cleaning companies do the work, send the invoice, and wait to be called. That is how a
£3,900 annual customer gets treated as a £150 transaction. The follow-up inside 48 hours,
asking directly whether they would like this kept up and offering a specific slot, is worth
more than any marketing you will run this year.

## Customer onboarding

The first visit sets the entire relationship. Treat it as a distinct process, not just the
first job.

**Before the first clean.**
- Confirm date, time window, and access the day before
- Confirm exactly what is included and what is not, in writing, again
- Set the honest expectation on a neglected property: the first clean brings it up to
  standard, and ongoing visits keep it there. Say this before the job, not after
- Tell them who is coming, by name

**During.**
- Cleaner arrives in the window, identifiable, with everything needed
- Photo evidence per the standard for that service
- Anything unexpected reported to the office the same day, not at the end of the week

**After, same day.**
- Completion notification with photos
- A named person checking the customer is happy

**Within 48 hours. The conversion conversation.**
- Ask what they thought, genuinely, and listen to the answer
- Fix anything raised, immediately and without argument
- Offer the recurring slot, specifically. A named day, a named time, the same cleaner
- If they say not yet, ask permission to check back in a month and put it in the system

## The lifecycle

After conversion, the relationship runs on a sequence. Automated where it can be, human
where it matters.

| When | Contact | Purpose |
|------|---------|---------|
| After every visit | Completion notification | Reassurance and evidence. Cheap, and it stops most small complaints becoming big ones |
| Day 7 after first clean | Satisfaction check | Catch problems while they are still fixable |
| Day 14 | Review request, if satisfied | Timed to the second visit, when the habit is forming |
| Day 30 | Add-on offer relevant to their property | Oven, carpets, windows, inside cupboards |
| Day 60 | Seasonal or service relevant contact | Useful, not salesy |
| Day 90 | Relationship check from a person, not a system | The one that catches quiet dissatisfaction |
| Every 6 months | Service review and price review | Both sides know where they stand |
| On a missed or skipped visit | Same-day contact | The single strongest churn signal in the business |
| 30 days dormant | Win-back sequence | Before they have settled in with someone else |

## Reviews

Reviews are the highest-return marketing asset a local cleaning company has. They compound,
they cost nothing, and they are the first thing a Coventry customer checks.

**The mechanics.**

- Ask after a visit that went well, not on a schedule that ignores how the job went
- The cleaner who did the work asks in person, the office follows with the link. An
  in-person ask followed by a link converts several times better than a link alone
- Make it one tap. A direct link to the Google review form, not to a homepage
- Google Business Profile first. It drives local search visibility and it is what people
  actually read. Facebook second. Trustpilot only if you will maintain it
- Respond to every review, positive and negative, in a human voice, within 48 hours
- A negative review gets an answer that shows a reader what kind of company this is. Do
  not argue. State what happened, what was done, and how to reach a person. The response
  is written for the two hundred people who read it, not for the reviewer

**Do not incentivise reviews.** Discounts, prize draws, and rewards for leaving a review
breach Google's policies and risk removal of reviews or worse. Ask well, ask at the right
moment, and ask often. That is enough, and it is durable.

**Chase the specifics.** A review that names the area, the service, and the cleaner is
worth several generic five-star ratings, for local search and for the next customer
reading it. When you ask, ask about the specific job, and the useful detail follows
naturally.

## Upsell

Not pressure. Relevance. A customer who has a fortnightly clean and does not know you do
ovens is a customer you have under-served.

| They have | Offer next | When |
|-----------|-----------|------|
| Regular domestic | Oven, carpets, windows, inside cupboards | Day 30, then seasonally |
| Regular domestic | Increase to weekly | When they mention the house getting away from them |
| Deep clean, one-off | Regular schedule | Within 48 hours. Always |
| End of tenancy, landlord | Turnaround agreement across their portfolio | At completion, while the quality is fresh |
| Office contract | Additional sites, periodic deep clean, carpet cycle, washroom consumables | At the six month review |
| Airbnb turnaround | Additional properties, linen service, deep clean cycle | Once reliability is proven, around month two |

**The landlord and agent route is the highest-leverage upsell in the business.** One
satisfied landlord with six properties is worth more than thirty domestic customers, costs
a fraction as much to acquire, and brings predictable repeat volume. When an end of
tenancy job goes well, that is the moment to ask what else they manage.

## Referral

Domestic cleaning referrals are strong because the recommendation comes with built-in trust
about someone entering a home.

- Ask at the right moment: after a compliment, after a good review, at the three month mark
- Make the reward mutual. Something for the referrer and something for the new customer.
  Account credit works better than cash and keeps the money in the business
- Never pay for referrals against a review. Keep the two entirely separate
- Track referral source on every lead, so you know which customers are advocates
- Thank every referral personally, whether or not it converts

A customer who has referred someone is significantly less likely to leave. The referral
programme is a retention mechanism as much as an acquisition one.

## Churn

**Detect it early.** By the time a customer cancels, they decided weeks ago. The leading
indicators, in order of reliability:

1. Skipping or postponing a scheduled visit
2. Reducing frequency
3. A complaint that was not fully resolved
4. A change of cleaner they did not ask for
5. Going quiet, having previously responded
6. A late or failed payment with no explanation

Any of these triggers a contact from a person within 24 hours. Not an automated message. A
call or a proper message from someone they know.

**The exit conversation.** When a customer does leave, ask why, once, and take the answer
at face value. Then ask whether they would like to be contacted in a few months. Roughly
one in ten dormant customers comes back when asked properly, and they cost nothing to
acquire the second time.

**Win-back.** After 30 days dormant, a short sequence: acknowledge it has been a while,
make a specific offer, make coming back easy. After 90 days, one last contact, then move
them to a quarterly nurture list. Do not hound people.

## Not every customer should be kept

This is the part most retention advice leaves out.

Some customers cost more than they are worth: chronically late payers, customers whose
properties consistently overrun, customers who complain unreasonably on every visit, and
customers who treat cleaners badly. That last one is not negotiable. A cleaner who is
disrespected in a customer's home and sees nothing done about it will leave, and you will
lose someone hard to replace in order to keep someone easy to replace.

Review the bottom of the customer list quarterly. Reprice, restructure, or release. Do it
politely, give reasonable notice, and do it. Capacity released from a bad customer is
capacity available for a good one.

## The records

- Full customer record per [reference/data-model.md](reference/data-model.md)
- Property details, preferences, access, and anything learned on site
- Complete service history with quality scores
- Every contact, from either side, logged
- Complaints and resolutions
- Review status: asked, left, platform, content
- Referrals given and received
- Lifetime value and current monthly value
- Status: active, at risk, dormant, released

## The numbers

| Number | Watch for |
|--------|-----------|
| First clean to recurring conversion | The headline number of this module |
| Monthly churn rate | Compounds brutally. A small improvement is worth more than a large marketing push |
| Customer lifetime value | Decides how much you can afford to spend acquiring one |
| Average customer value per month | Rising through upsell, not just price increases |
| Review volume and rating trend | Volume matters as much as average |
| Referral rate | Percentage of customers who have referred at least once |
| Reactivation rate | How well win-back actually works |
| Revenue concentration | What share of revenue sits with your largest customer. Above a quarter is a risk, not a success |

## Definition of done

- [ ] Every first clean triggers a conversion conversation inside 48 hours, without exception
- [ ] The lifecycle sequence runs automatically and does not depend on anyone remembering
- [ ] Reviews are asked for in person and followed with a one-tap link
- [ ] Every review gets a response within 48 hours
- [ ] Skipped visits trigger a human contact within 24 hours
- [ ] Upsell offers are matched to what the customer actually has
- [ ] A referral scheme exists, is tracked, and is separate from review requests
- [ ] The bottom of the customer list is reviewed quarterly and acted on
