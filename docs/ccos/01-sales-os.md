# 01 — Sales OS

## What it owns

Everything from the moment a stranger raises their hand to the moment a job is in the
diary with a price on it. Response, qualification, pricing, quoting, follow-up,
objection handling, and the close.

Sales OS does not own marketing spend or channel strategy. It owns what happens to
every lead those channels produce, and it owns the price.

## The rule that makes this work

Quote the recurring price first.

Every quote leads with the fortnightly figure. The one-off price is shown second and
it is higher. Not as a trick, as an honest reflection of cost: a first clean on a
neglected property takes longer, and a customer we see twice a month costs less to
serve than one we see once.

This single sequencing decision is the difference between a transaction business and
an asset business. A customer who says yes to £150 once is worth £150. A customer who
says yes to £150 a fortnight is worth roughly £3,900 a year. The words are almost
identical. The business is not.

## Response rules

Speed beats polish. The Coventry cleaning market is fragmented and most local
competitors answer enquiries when they get round to it. Answering first, properly, wins
work that a better cleaner loses.

| Channel | Target first response | Maximum |
|---------|----------------------|---------|
| Phone, during hours | Answered live | 3 rings |
| Missed call | Callback or text | 5 minutes |
| WhatsApp | 5 minutes | 30 minutes |
| Website form | 10 minutes | 1 hour |
| Google Business message | 10 minutes | 1 hour |
| Email | 1 hour | 4 hours |
| Facebook / Instagram DM | 30 minutes | 4 hours |
| Out of hours, any channel | Automated acknowledgement within 2 minutes, human by 09:00 | 09:30 |

Operating hours for response purposes: 08:00 to 19:00 Monday to Friday, 09:00 to 16:00
Saturday. Outside those, the acknowledgement goes out automatically and says when a
person will come back. Never leave silence. Silence is where the lead goes and books a
competitor.

**The missed call rule.** Every missed call gets a text inside five minutes. Something
plain: a line saying we missed them, who we are, and asking what they need doing and
where. A missed call with no follow-up is money already spent on marketing, thrown away.

## Qualification

Nine things. Gather them in conversation, not as an interrogation. If you have the
first four you can price most domestic work.

1. **Postcode.** Determines travel, route fit, and whether we serve it at all.
2. **Service type.** Regular, deep, end of tenancy, office, post-build, Airbnb turnaround.
3. **Property size.** Bedrooms and bathrooms for domestic. Floor area and room count for commercial.
4. **Frequency wanted.** Weekly, fortnightly, monthly, one-off. Ask even if they said one-off.
5. **Condition.** When was it last cleaned properly. This is the single biggest driver of time on a first visit.
6. **Access.** Keys, in-person, key safe, agent collection, parking situation.
7. **Date needed.** Tells you urgency, which tells you price sensitivity.
8. **Pets and occupants.** Affects time, allergens, and which cleaner is suitable.
9. **How they found us.** One question, logged every time. Without it you cannot calculate cost per acquisition in 05.

**Never quote a deep clean or end of tenancy on bedroom count alone.** Condition drives
the hours, and the gap between a tidy two-bed and a neglected two-bed can be six hours.
Ask for photos. Most customers send them without complaint. A quote given blind on a
neglected property is a loss you agreed to in advance.

## Service areas

| Tier | Coverage | Rule |
|------|----------|------|
| Core | CV1 to CV6, plus CV7 where it borders the city | Standard pricing. Always yes if capacity allows |
| Extended | CV8 Kenilworth, CV11 Nuneaton, CV21 to CV23 Rugby, CV31 to CV34 Leamington and Warwick | Minimum job value applies. Prefer clustering on one day |
| Outside | Everywhere else | Only for contract work above the commercial minimum, and only with a route that makes sense |

Set a minimum job value for Extended and hold it. One isolated job forty minutes away,
priced as though it were round the corner, eats a cleaner's whole morning in travel. Two
of those a week is a lost day of capacity.

## Pricing

Pricing rules live here. The cost inputs that set them live in
[05 Finance OS](05-finance-os.md). Do not set a price in this module without checking
it clears the margin floor in 05.

**Structure of the grid.** Build one table per service, priced by the driver that
actually determines time:

| Service | Priced by | Frequency discount |
|---------|-----------|--------------------|
| CC-HOME-01 Regular clean | Bedrooms + bathrooms, fixed hours | Weekly best, fortnightly next, monthly least |
| CC-HOME-02 Deep clean | Bedrooms + bathrooms + condition band | None, one-off by nature |
| CC-HOME-03 End of tenancy | Bedrooms + bathrooms + oven/carpets as add-ons | None |
| CC-COM-01 Office standard | Floor area and visit frequency | Contract length, not frequency |
| CC-COM-02 Commercial deep | Quoted per site after survey | None |
| CC-PROP-01 Landlord turnaround | Property size, volume agreement | Volume committed per month |
| CC-STR-01 Airbnb / short let | Property size, fixed turnaround fee, linen as add-on | Volume per month |

**Three rules that hold across the whole grid.**

1. **Condition bands, not guesses.** Light, standard, neglected. Each band carries a
   multiplier you set once. Written down, the same for every quote, applied without
   negotiation. This stops the two failures that kill deep clean margin: underquoting a
   bad property, and overquoting a clean one and losing it.
2. **First clean is separate.** A recurring customer's first visit is priced as its own
   job, usually at deep or intermediate rate, then the ongoing rate applies. Say this
   plainly at quote stage. A customer who finds out afterwards feels sold to.
3. **The floor is a floor.** Below the margin floor in 05, the answer is no. Not a
   smaller discount. No. A job at break-even consumes the capacity that a profitable job
   needed, and it sets the price you will be held to at renewal.

**Commercial and contract pricing** always follows a site visit. Never quote an office,
a care setting, or a post-construction job from a phone description. The survey is not a
cost, it is the thing that stops you signing a twelve month contract at the wrong number.

## The quote

Sent within 2 hours for standard domestic work, 24 hours for anything needing a survey.

Every quote contains, in this order:

1. What we will do, room by room, in plain language. Not "full clean of property".
2. How long it will take and how many cleaners.
3. **The recurring price first.** Fortnightly, then weekly if cheaper per visit, then
   monthly.
4. The one-off price, second, and higher.
5. First clean price if different, stated separately.
6. What is not included, and the price to add it. Ovens, carpets, windows, inside
   cupboards.
7. Valid for 14 days.
8. One line of proof. A review from the same area, or a number, or the insurance and
   vetting position. Something that answers why trust us rather than the cheaper name.
9. One clear next step. Not "let me know". A named date and time we can hold.

Keep it short enough to read on a phone in ninety seconds. Long quotes do not convert
better, they convert slower.

## Follow-up

Most cleaning companies send a quote and wait. The money is in the follow-up, and the
follow-up should be a sequence, not a mood.

| When | Action | Purpose |
|------|--------|---------|
| +2 hours | Check the quote arrived, offer to answer anything | Catch spam folders and confusion |
| +24 hours | One message, offer two specific slots | Make saying yes a small decision |
| +72 hours | Answer the likely objection before they raise it | Price, trust, or timing |
| +7 days | Availability nudge, name the slot going | Real scarcity only, never invented |
| +21 days | Last contact on this quote, offer to requote | Clean close |
| Then | Move to nurture list, contact quarterly | Long cycle leads still convert |

Stop immediately when they say no or ask you to stop. Log the reason. The lost reasons
column is the most useful column in the whole CRM, because it tells you whether you have
a pricing problem, a trust problem, or a speed problem.

## Objections

Three cover most of it. Answer the concern, do not argue with the person.

**"That's more than I expected."** Do not discount first. Ask what they were comparing
against, then either restructure the job, reduce frequency, reduce scope, or let it go.
Restructuring holds the rate. Discounting sets a new rate forever, and they will tell
their neighbour what they paid.

**"I'll have a think."** Almost always means an unspoken concern. Ask directly and
plainly which part they are unsure about, and offer a smaller first commitment, a single
clean before committing to regular. Low-risk entry converts thinkers.

**"My cleaner charges twelve pounds an hour."** They are comparing a private individual
to a business. Say plainly what the difference buys: insurance, a vetted and trained
cleaner, cover when someone is ill, materials, a standard that is checked, and someone
to call if it goes wrong. Do not criticise the other cleaner. Never compete on hourly
rate against cash work. You will lose, and the customer you win that way leaves at the
first price rise.

## Booking

The job is not sold until all of this exists:

- Confirmed date, time window, and duration
- Confirmed price and what it covers
- Access arrangement agreed and recorded
- Terms accepted, including the cancellation policy
- Payment method set up
- Customer record created with everything from qualification
- Handover to Operations, so the job appears in the schedule with the scope attached

**Cancellation policy.** Publish it, apply it evenly. A clear window, a stated charge
inside it, and one goodwill exception per customer. The reason this matters is not the
fee. It is that a cleaner booked for a cancelled job still needs paying, and without a
policy that cost lands entirely on you.

**Deposits.** Take one on deep cleans, end of tenancy, and anything over a threshold you
set. No-shows on high-value one-offs cost a full day of capacity.

## The records

Every lead gets a record from first contact, whether it converts or not. Fields in
[reference/data-model.md](reference/data-model.md). Minimum:

- Source, date, channel, first response time
- All nine qualification answers
- Quote sent, amount, service, date
- Every follow-up contact, logged
- Outcome: won, lost, nurture. If lost, why, in their words

A lead with no source is a marketing decision you cannot make. A loss with no reason is
a lesson you paid for and did not collect.

## The numbers

Defined in [reference/kpi-dictionary.md](reference/kpi-dictionary.md).

| Number | Why it matters |
|--------|----------------|
| Speed to first response, median | The one you can fix fastest and the one that moves conversion most |
| Enquiry to quote rate | Low means qualification is leaking or you are too slow |
| Quote to booking conversion | The health of your price and your follow-up together |
| Recurring share of new bookings | The asset-building number. Target the majority |
| Average job value | Tells you whether you are winning the right work |
| Cost per qualified lead, by channel | Decides where the marketing money goes next month |
| Lost reason distribution | Tells you which problem to fix |

Watch conversion and average value together. Conversion rising while average value falls
means you are discounting your way to a busy, poor year.

## Definition of done

- [ ] Response time targets are written, known, and measured for every channel
- [ ] Missed calls trigger a text inside five minutes, automatically
- [ ] The nine qualification questions are captured on every enquiry, in one place
- [ ] A pricing grid exists on paper, with condition bands, checked against the 05 margin floor
- [ ] Quotes lead with the recurring price, every time, without exception
- [ ] The follow-up sequence runs whether or not anyone remembers it
- [ ] Every lead has a source and every loss has a reason
- [ ] Someone other than Taka could quote a standard domestic job correctly from the grid alone
