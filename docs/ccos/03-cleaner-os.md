# 03 — Cleaner OS

## What it owns

Who cleans for Coventry Cleans and how good they are. Recruitment, vetting, onboarding,
training, certification, pay structure, performance management, progression, retention,
and exit.

Operations takes the people this module produces. If Operations is constantly firefighting
capability problems, the fault is here.

## The rule that makes this work

Retention is a margin lever, not an HR nicety.

Cleaning has some of the highest staff turnover of any sector. Most operators accept it as
weather. It is not weather, it is a cost, and it is one of the largest uncontrolled costs
in the business. Every cleaner who leaves takes with them: the advertising spend to find
them, the hours spent screening and inducting, the training investment, the productivity
gap while a replacement gets up to speed, and the customer relationships they held. On
recurring domestic work, a departing cleaner frequently takes a customer's loyalty with
them even when the customer stays.

Work it out once, honestly, in your own numbers. Put that figure on the wall. Then treat
every decision that affects whether a good cleaner stays as a financial decision, because
it is.

## Recruitment

**Recruit continuously, not reactively.** The worst hiring decisions in this industry are
made in the week you lost someone and have jobs to cover on Monday. Keep a live pipeline
even at full capacity. A bench of one or two vetted people you have already met is the
cheapest insurance you can buy.

**Where they come from.** Referrals from existing cleaners convert best and stay longest;
pay for them, and pay after the new starter has completed a qualifying period rather than
on day one. Beyond that: local Facebook groups, Indeed, community noticeboards, and
word of mouth in the areas you already serve. A cleaner who lives in the postcode cluster
they will work in is worth more than a better cleaner who has to cross the city.

**The advert.** Say what most cleaning adverts will not. Real hourly rate, realistic hours
per week, the areas, whether travel is paid, how and when pay arrives, what training is
provided, and what progression exists. Vagueness attracts applicants who leave when they
discover the specifics. Specificity attracts fewer applicants and better ones.

**Screening, in order.**

1. Phone call, ten minutes. Availability, area, transport, right to work, experience.
2. Face to face, thirty minutes. Reliability history, why they left the last role, how they
   handle a customer complaint, whether they are comfortable working alone in homes.
3. Paid trial shift alongside a Team Leader or experienced cleaner. This is the real
   interview. Almost everything you need to know shows up in three hours of actual work:
   pace, thoroughness, how they treat someone's home, whether they ask questions, whether
   they finish.
4. Two references, at least one from a previous employer where possible.

Never skip the trial shift because you are busy. Being busy is exactly when a bad hire
does the most damage.

**Vetting, without exception.**

- Right to work check, documented and retained. This is a legal obligation with civil
  penalties running to tens of thousands of pounds per worker, and the defence is the
  documented check you did before they started. Verify the current process and penalty
  levels with your accountant or the current Home Office guidance, not from memory.
- DBS check where the work involves healthcare settings, vulnerable people, or
  unsupervised access. Make the vetting standard part of your public positioning.
- Identity and address confirmation for every cleaner holding customer keys.

## Employment status

Read this alongside [05 Finance OS](05-finance-os.md) before setting up any cleaner as
self-employed.

Much of the UK cleaning industry engages cleaners as self-employed contractors. It is
cheaper on paper. It is also the area where small cleaning companies are most often wrong,
because status is determined by the reality of the arrangement, not by what the contract
says or what both parties preferred.

The arrangement starts to look like employment when the company sets the schedule, provides
the uniform, provides the materials and equipment, sets the method and the standards,
requires the person to attend personally rather than send a substitute, and is their main
or only source of work. Most cleaning businesses that want consistent quality end up doing
every one of those things, because that is what delivering a standard requires.

Decide deliberately which model you are running, take proper advice on it, and build the
operation to match. A misclassification discovered later brings back-dated tax, National
Insurance, holiday pay, and penalties, and it lands at the worst possible moment: when the
business is large enough to be worth looking at.

## Coventry Cleans Academy

Six levels. Each one is training, then assessment, then certification, then a rate and a
scope of work that goes with it. A level that carries no pay difference and no additional
work is a badge, and cleaners see through badges.

| Level | Title | Covers | Typical time to reach |
|-------|-------|--------|----------------------|
| 1 | CC Foundation | Induction, brand standards, health and safety, COSHH basics, PPE, customer conduct, lone working, the job pack, how to use the app | Day one to week one |
| 2 | Residential Certified | CC-HOME-01 to full standard, room sequences, products and dilutions, time standards, photo evidence | Weeks 2 to 6 |
| 3 | Commercial Certified | CC-COM-01, site protocols, working around staff and the public, sign-in procedures, contract expectations | Month 3 onward |
| 4 | Deep Clean Specialist | CC-HOME-02 and CC-HOME-03, condition assessment, oven and limescale work, end of tenancy evidence standards, deposit-dispute awareness | Month 6 onward |
| 5 | Team Leader | Leading two to four cleaners, inspections and scoring, on-site training, first-line complaint handling, escalation judgement | Month 9 onward |
| 6 | Operations Manager | Scheduling, capacity, quality system ownership, recruitment, performance conversations | By merit |

**Anchor the Academy to a recognised external standard.** The British Institute of
Cleaning Science operates the recognised UK training and skills framework for the industry.
Building your internal levels so they map to it, and putting cleaners through external
assessment as they progress, gives you three things at once: better cleaning, a genuine
credential the cleaner keeps and values, and a credibility advantage on commercial and
healthcare tenders where buyers ask what training your staff hold. Check current course
structures and costs directly rather than assuming.

**Certification is assessed, not attended.** Watching a video is not Level 2. A supervised
job scored against the standard is Level 2. If the certificate can be obtained by sitting
through something, it will not change how anyone cleans.

## Pay

Get this right before anything else in the module. Cleaners leave over pay being wrong or
late far more often than over pay being low.

**The rules:**

- Meet or exceed the National Living Wage for the cleaner's age band, and verify the
  current rate at every April uprating rather than relying on last year's figure.
- Pay travel time between jobs, or build it into the rate transparently. Cleaners who feel
  they work unpaid hours between jobs leave, and they tell others why.
- Holiday accrues at 12.07% of hours worked for irregular-hours workers, and it is a real
  cost that must be in your price. Companies that forget it believe they are running a 40%
  margin when they are running considerably less. Check the current rules on rolled-up
  holiday pay before choosing how to administer it.
- Pension auto-enrolment applies once earnings cross the trigger. Budget the employer
  contribution from the start, not as a surprise.
- Employer National Insurance is a material cost per employed cleaner and the thresholds
  move. Model it at current rates, not remembered ones.
- Pay on the same day every period, without fail. This is the cheapest loyalty you will
  ever buy and the most expensive thing to get wrong.

**Progression pay.** Each Academy level carries a rate step. Publish the steps. A cleaner
who can see the path from Level 2 to Level 5, with the money attached, has a reason to
stay that a competitor offering fifty pence more an hour cannot match.

**Performance pay, carefully.** Bonuses tied to the Quality Score work. Bonuses tied to
speed do not, because speed is exactly the wrong thing to incentivise in cleaning. If you
pay for anything beyond the rate, pay for quality scores, customer retention on their
accounts, reliability, and completed certifications.

## Performance

Driven by the Quality Score from [02 Operations OS](02-operations-os.md), reviewed monthly.

| Situation | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| Consistently Elite | Say so, publicly. Offer progression, better accounts, and a lead role |
| Consistently Good | Nothing. Leave competent people alone. Over-managing good cleaners drives them out |
| Improvement band | Team Leader conversation inside a week. One specific fix, written down, a date to review |
| Intervention band | A plan, not a warning: retraining on the specific gap, a supervised visit, and a named review date |
| Plan fails | Formal process, properly documented, taken with advice |
| Reliability failure | Treated more seriously than a quality failure. A cleaner who does not turn up costs a customer. One who cleans imperfectly costs a re-clean |

**Monthly one to one, fifteen minutes, every cleaner.** Their scores, anything they need,
anything not working, and one thing they want to learn. Most cleaning companies never do
this, which is precisely why doing it is worth so much. People leave managers who never
ask.

## Retention

What actually keeps good cleaners, in rough order of impact:

1. Consistent, predictable hours. Unpredictable weeks make the job unlivable
2. Pay that is correct and on time, every time
3. Being told about changes before they happen, not after
4. Equipment and products that work, supplied without a fight
5. Being treated as skilled, not disposable
6. A route that makes sense and does not waste their day in traffic
7. A manager who notices good work out loud
8. A visible path to more money

Notice how little of that is the hourly rate. Rate matters up to the point of being fair.
Beyond that, the list above decides who stays.

**Exit interviews, always.** Ten minutes, honest, no consequences. Ask what would have kept
them. Log it. Three cleaners naming the same thing is a system fault and it will keep
costing you until it is fixed.

## Definition of done

- [ ] Every cleaner has a documented right to work check, retained and in date
- [ ] A paid trial shift happens before every hire, without exception
- [ ] Employment status has been decided deliberately, with advice, and the operation matches it
- [ ] Levels 1 and 2 of the Academy exist as real assessed content, not intentions
- [ ] Every level has a published rate step attached
- [ ] Holiday accrual, pension, and employer NI are in the cost model in 05
- [ ] Monthly one to ones happen for every cleaner
- [ ] Exit interviews are conducted and logged
- [ ] A recruitment pipeline exists when you are at full capacity, not only when you are short
