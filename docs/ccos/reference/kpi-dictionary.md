# Reference — KPI Dictionary

One definition per number. The point of this file is that when two people say "conversion
rate" in the same meeting, they mean the same thing.

No targets are given for most of these, deliberately. Measure your own baseline for a month
first, then set a target you can actually move. A target copied from a franchise brochure
tells you nothing about your business.

## How to read this

Every entry has a formula, a measurement period, an owner, and a note on what it is really
telling you. Where a number can be gamed, that is said plainly, because every number in a
service business can be gamed and the ones that get reported are the ones that get gamed.

## Sales

### Speed to first response
**Formula:** median minutes from lead created to first human or automated response
**Period:** weekly · **Owner:** Sales
Use the median, not the mean. One enquiry answered three days late drags a mean so far that
the number stops describing normal performance. Break it down by channel, because the fix
is usually specific to one.

### Enquiry to quote rate
**Formula:** quotes sent ÷ qualified enquiries
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Sales
Low means qualification is leaking or you are too slow. Distinguish the two before
reacting, because the fixes are opposite.

### Quote to booking conversion
**Formula:** bookings ÷ quotes sent, measured on quotes sent in the period
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Sales
Cohort it by when the quote was sent, not when it converted, or a good month will borrow
credit from the previous one.
**Gaming risk:** quoting only easy jobs. Watch alongside enquiry to quote rate.

### Recurring share of new bookings
**Formula:** new recurring bookings ÷ all new bookings
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Sales
The asset-building number. If it is falling while total bookings rise, the business is
getting busier without getting more valuable.

### Average job value
**Formula:** total booked revenue ÷ number of jobs booked
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Sales
Read it next to conversion, always. Conversion up and average value down means you are
discounting your way to a busy, poor year.

### Cost per qualified lead
**Formula:** channel spend ÷ qualified leads from that channel
**Period:** monthly, by channel · **Owner:** Marketing
Include time cost on channels that eat hours rather than money. Organic is not free.

### Customer acquisition cost
**Formula:** total sales and marketing spend ÷ new customers won
**Period:** monthly, by channel · **Owner:** Finance
By channel, or it cannot inform a decision. Blended acquisition cost tells you the past;
channel-level tells you where next month's money goes.

## Operations

### On-time arrival rate
**Formula:** jobs where check-in fell within the promised window ÷ completed jobs
**Period:** weekly · **Owner:** Operations
The service failure customers forgive least, because it costs them their morning.

### Utilisation
**Formula:** booked cleaner hours ÷ available cleaner hours
**Period:** weekly · **Owner:** Operations
Too low means you are paying for idle capacity. Too high means no resilience against
illness or overrun. Set a band, not a maximum.

### Productive hour ratio
**Formula:** hours charged to customers ÷ hours paid to cleaners
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Finance
The most under-measured number in cleaning, and one of the most valuable. It converts
directly into margin through the cost model in [05](../05-finance-os.md). Improving it
raises profit on work you already have.

### Jobs per cleaner per day
**Formula:** completed jobs ÷ cleaner days worked
**Period:** weekly · **Owner:** Operations
Rising is good only if quality holds. Read it beside the quality score, never alone.
**Gaming risk:** the fastest way to raise this number is to clean less thoroughly.

### Average quality score
**Formula:** mean CC Quality Score across completed jobs
**Period:** weekly · **Owner:** Quality
Trend matters more than level. Report the distribution too. An average of 85 made of 95s
and 70s is a completely different business from one where everything is 85.

### Complaint rate
**Formula:** complaints ÷ completed jobs × 100
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Quality
The honest measure of whether the service standards work.
**Gaming risk:** discouraging complaints, or resolving them informally without logging
them. A falling complaint rate alongside a falling review rating means complaints are being
suppressed, not prevented.

### Re-clean rate
**Formula:** jobs requiring a free re-clean ÷ completed jobs
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Quality
The direct cost of quality failure. Multiply by average job cost for the real figure and
put that number in front of the quality conversation.

### Overrun rate by service code
**Formula:** jobs exceeding allowed time by more than 20% ÷ jobs on that code
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Operations
Where pricing and reality have drifted apart. Persistent overrun on one code means the time
standard in 02 or the price in 01 is wrong. Fix the standard, not the cleaner.

### Continuity rate
**Formula:** recurring visits done by the customer's usual cleaner ÷ recurring visits
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Operations
Leads churn by roughly a month. A falling continuity rate today is a rising churn rate
next quarter.

## People

### 90 day retention
**Formula:** starters still with the business at 90 days ÷ starters in that cohort
**Period:** monthly by cohort · **Owner:** People
The honest measure of recruitment and onboarding together. Most cleaning turnover happens
in the first twelve weeks.

### 12 month retention
**Formula:** cleaners still with the business 12 months after starting ÷ that cohort
**Period:** monthly by cohort · **Owner:** People
Measures whether the job is worth keeping, as opposed to worth starting.

### Cost per hire
**Formula:** (advertising + screening time + trial shift cost + induction cost) ÷ hires
**Period:** quarterly · **Owner:** People
Put the real number on the wall. It changes how retention decisions get made.

### Time to Level 2
**Formula:** median days from start to Residential Certified
**Period:** quarterly · **Owner:** People
How quickly a new starter becomes genuinely useful. Directly limits how fast you can grow.

### Certified cleaner count by level
**Formula:** headcount at each Academy level
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** People
Your real capacity for deep cleans, commercial, and contract work. Sales should know this
number before promising a contract start date.

### Absence and no-show rate
**Formula:** unplanned absences ÷ scheduled shifts
**Period:** weekly · **Owner:** Operations
The leading indicator of someone about to leave, usually by several weeks.

### Revenue per cleaner
**Formula:** total revenue ÷ average cleaners in period
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Finance
Where people and finance meet. Rising means better density, pricing, or skill. Falling
while headcount grows means you are hiring ahead of demand.

## Customer

### First clean to recurring conversion
**Formula:** first cleans that became recurring ÷ first cleans completed
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Customer
The headline number of [04](../04-customer-os.md) and one of the highest-leverage figures
in the business.

### Monthly churn rate
**Formula:** recurring customers lost in the month ÷ recurring customers at the start
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Customer
Compounds brutally. Average customer lifespan in months is roughly 1 ÷ monthly churn rate.
At 10% that is ten months; at 5% it is twenty.

### Customer lifetime value
**Formula:** average monthly contribution × average lifespan in months
**Period:** quarterly · **Owner:** Finance
Use contribution, not revenue. Revenue-based lifetime value flatters and misleads, and it
will justify marketing spend you cannot actually afford.

### Lifetime value to acquisition cost ratio
**Formula:** customer lifetime value ÷ customer acquisition cost
**Period:** quarterly, by channel · **Owner:** Finance
Below 3, growth is expensive and fragile. Well above 3 with spare capacity means you are
under-investing in marketing.

### Payback period
**Formula:** customer acquisition cost ÷ average monthly contribution
**Period:** quarterly · **Owner:** Finance
Months to get the acquisition cost back. In a cash-tight business this constrains growth
more than the ratio does.

### Average customer value
**Formula:** total monthly recurring revenue ÷ active recurring customers
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Customer
Should rise through upsell and frequency, not only through price increases.

### Review volume and rating
**Formula:** new reviews in period, and running average by platform
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Marketing
Volume matters as much as average. A 4.8 from two hundred reviews outperforms a 5.0 from
nine, both in search and in the mind of the person reading them.

### Referral rate
**Formula:** customers who have referred at least once ÷ active customers
**Period:** quarterly · **Owner:** Customer
Also a retention indicator. Customers who refer stay longer.

### Reactivation rate
**Formula:** dormant customers returned ÷ dormant customers contacted
**Period:** quarterly · **Owner:** Customer

## Finance

### Contribution margin
**Formula:** (revenue − fully loaded labour − materials − travel) ÷ revenue
**Period:** per job, aggregated monthly by service code and by customer
**Owner:** Finance
The number the margin floor is set against. Fully loaded labour means after holiday
accrual, employer National Insurance, and pension, not the headline hourly rate.

### Gross margin
**Formula:** (revenue − all direct delivery costs) ÷ revenue
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Finance
Contribution aggregated across the business. Track the trend; a slow decline usually means
price drift rather than a single bad decision.

### Monthly recurring revenue
**Formula:** sum of each recurring customer's agreed price × visits per month
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Finance
For fortnightly customers, multiply by 26 and divide by 12 rather than assuming two visits
a month. The difference is a fortnight of revenue a year and it matters at scale.

### Recurring share of revenue
**Formula:** recurring revenue ÷ total revenue
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Finance
The single best measure of whether this is becoming an asset business or staying a
transaction business.

### Rolling twelve month turnover
**Formula:** total taxable turnover across the last 12 months, recalculated monthly
**Period:** monthly · **Owner:** Finance
The VAT threshold test. Rolling, not tax year. Review it every month without fail. See
[05](../05-finance-os.md).

### Aged debt
**Formula:** outstanding invoice value by age bracket: current, 14, 30, 60, 90+ days
**Period:** weekly · **Owner:** Finance
Watch the movement between brackets, not just the total. Money sliding from 14 to 30 is the
early warning.

### Cash runway
**Formula:** available cash ÷ average weekly outgoings, in weeks
**Period:** weekly · **Owner:** Finance
Below its floor, this becomes the constraint of the month in
[00](../00-architecture.md) and nothing else gets attention.

### Revenue concentration
**Formula:** largest customer's revenue ÷ total revenue
**Period:** quarterly · **Owner:** Finance
Above a quarter from one customer is a risk wearing the costume of a success. Commercial
contracts are excellent until the one that is a third of your revenue retenders.

## Reporting cadence summary

| Period | Numbers |
|--------|---------|
| Daily | Jobs scheduled and staffed, exceptions, new leads, cash in |
| Weekly | Leads, quotes, bookings, conversions, quality below 80, open complaints, cash, aged debt, utilisation, on-time rate |
| Monthly | Everything marked monthly, plus P&L against plan |
| Quarterly | Unit economics, customer profitability, pricing review, cost per hire, concentration |

Anything measured less often than quarterly is not a KPI. It is a fact you look up
occasionally, and it should not be on the dashboard competing for attention.
